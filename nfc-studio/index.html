The fiddly parts are already handled.

A pretty preview is easy. A card that comes off the plate right the first time takes a few details most logo-to-STL tools skip.

Sealed tag
The tag ends up fully enclosed. No sticker or recess on the back. The cavity is built to your tag's real thickness, so the cover prints straight onto the tag instead of sagging over open air. Prefer an open pocket? Untick one box.
Pause layer
It tells you exactly where to stop. For example Z = 1.80 mm, after layer 9. The cavity's floor and ceiling are snapped to real layer boundaries for your layer and first-layer heights, so the pause lands cleanly and the back stays flat.
AMS slots
Every file has a slot. The card body is AMS 1, logo colours are AMS 2–4. Sample colours straight off your logo, or lock your own palette. A fourth logo colour is supported too, and the tool warns you that it needs a fifth filament.
Face-down
The card prints face-down, so the export is mirrored for you. The logo is the first layers against the plate, which is why a textured plate becomes the card's finish. When you peel it off and turn it over, it reads the right way round. Don't rotate it.
Watertight
Closed, manifold meshes. Colour parts drop exactly into recesses in the card, with no gaps or overlaps. Bambu Studio shouldn't ask to repair them.
Colour swaps
Colour only lives in the first few layers. 0.6 mm of colour is three 0.2 mm layers, so filament changes, and purge, stop early. Fill the plate with cards and you pay that purge once per layer, not once per card.

How it works

From logo file to a card in your hand.

  1. Drop in your logo

    PNG, JPG, SVG or WEBP. A transparent PNG or an SVG traces best. The background colour is dropped automatically.

  2. Check the colours

    It separates the art into flat colours and shows which AMS slot each one goes to. Lower the count to lose a stray shade, sample a colour off the logo, or lock your own.

  3. Pick a card or keychain

    Choose a preset and a tag. Add company info as real geometry if you like: up to three lines, printed in one of your logo colours.

  4. Build and download

    Build the mesh, spin the 3D preview, then download the STL set as a .zip.

  5. Load it into Bambu Studio In your slicer

    Drag all the STLs onto the plate at once and answer Yes to loading them as one object with multiple parts. Set each part's filament to the slot the tool gave it.

  6. Add the pause, print, drop in the tag At the printer

    Right-click the layer slider at the height the tool reported and choose Add pause. When it stops, press the tag flat in the cavity and resume. The next layers close over it.

3D preview

After Build mesh you're looking at the actual solids that go into the zip, not a picture of them: drag to turn the card over, scroll to zoom. The status line reports the part and triangle counts.

What's in the download

One STL per filament, and the notes to print them.

  • One solid per filament. STL holds no colour, so a closed solid per filament is how a multi-colour part is described. A three-colour logo gives four files.
  • All parts share one origin. Load them together and they line up exactly. Don't move them individually.
  • A README with your numbers. Card size, colour depth, cavity size, the pause height and layer, the AMS slot for each file, and import and slicing steps.
  • A 3MF button for other slicers. It's labelled single-colour for Bambu, for a reason: see the FAQ.

FAQ

Questions makers ask.

Which NFC tags should I buy?

Round NTAG215 stickers, 25 mm, are the default for cards. Keychains use 22 mm (an NTAG213 sticker), 20 mm on the pill. The tool also has presets for 30 mm stickers and a 45 × 25 mm rectangular wet inlay, or you can enter any size. NTAG215 holds 504 bytes, enough for a full contact card; NTAG213 holds 144, fine for a link. Measure your tag's thickness with calipers if you can and enter it: a bare sticker is about 0.3–0.4 mm, a foam-backed disc nearer 0.8 mm.

Does it work with OrcaSlicer?

NFC Studio is built and tested against Bambu Studio. OrcaSlicer is based on Bambu Studio and imports multiple STLs the same way: select them all, load them as one object with multiple parts, then set each part's filament. You add the pause from the layer slider in the preview, as in Bambu Studio. Use the STL set, not the 3MF.

Why STL files and not a 3MF?

Bambu Studio only keeps part and filament data from 3MF files it wrote itself. Anything else gets the message "The 3mf is not from Bambu Lab, load geometry data and color data only" and is flattened into one object with one filament. That holds even when the file matches Bambu's own layout, which ours does. The only way around it is to embed a full printer and filament profile, which would overwrite your print settings. Loading the STL set as one object with multiple parts is the reliable path, so that's what NFC Studio gives you.

Do I need an AMS?

You need a printer that can print several filaments in the same layer: an AMS, AMS Lite, or similar. A manual colour change at a layer won't work, because the logo sits in the same layers as the card face. Card plus three logo colours fills a four-slot AMS.

How do I program the tag?

With your phone and a free NFC app such as NFC Tools. Write a link, a contact card, or whatever you want a tap to open. Writing it before you drop it in lets you check it works. It also works afterwards, through the printed cover. Most apps can lock a tag so it can't be rewritten; that's permanent, so lock it last. Keep finished cards off metal surfaces, which detune the antenna.

Is my logo uploaded anywhere?

No. Tracing, meshing and the zip are all done in your browser. Your logo never leaves your machine. The only network call is the one-time license check when you unlock Pro.

Can I sell what I print?

With Commercial, yes: cards and keychains you print, and files you make for clients. Free and Pro are for personal use.
