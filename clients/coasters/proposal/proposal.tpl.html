02 What I looked at — and what I couldn't

Straight about the homework.

Normally this section is a measured audit of your current site: how many requests it makes, how heavy the page is, which security headers are set. I run that with a tool, not by eye. This time I couldn't run it — the machine I was working from couldn't reach coasterspubngrill.com, so I'm not going to print numbers I didn't measure. Here's only what I could actually verify from public listings:

Page title
Your homepage title reads "Coasters - Best American food in Racine, WI - Pizza Near You." That's ordering-platform boilerplate, not your name. It's also the line Google shows people.
Platform
The site has a /cart route and generic SEO phrasing — the signature of a templated ordering platform, where your bar gets the same page as every other restaurant on it.
Listings
Google, Yelp, Tripadvisor, Grubhub, Facebook and a dozen scraper sites all carry a version of your menu. Several disagree with each other.
Rating
Roughly 4.3 on Google as reported across listing sites. The food and the room get credit; service complaints are the recurring theme in the low ones.
Why the title matters. "Pizza Near You" is a phrase a template wrote. It doesn't say Racine's corner bar since 1995, it doesn't say eleven flavors of wings, and it doesn't say Friday fish fry — which are the three reasons someone picks you. Why the platform matters. On a templated ordering site you're renting a page shaped like everyone else's. You can't put your fish fry above the fold, you can't run your own events, and the design isn't yours to change. Why the disagreeing listings matter. When your hours or menu differ between your site and Google, Google trusts itself. People show up at the wrong time and blame you. What I'd still like to run. A real measured audit of the live site — requests, page weight, security headers — plus the free Google Business Profile score. Both are free, both take me an afternoon, and I'd send you the numbers whether or not you hire me.
