Social Care

Pricing, deliverables and production workflow for managing a client's social accounts — derived from the rates already published on the Veer rate card, so the two never contradict each other. Full working notes in SPEC.md.

01 Where it fits

The rung that was advertised and never priced

The published rate card already ends on this promise — step 3 of the “What’s next” ladder reads “AI working behind the scenes — chases invoices, replies to reviews, and drafts your posts.” Social isn’t a new direction. It’s the rung clients are already being shown.

The number that sets the floor

Google Care is $85/mo for 8 posts to a single surface, with a 48-hour reply SLA and a monthly re-score. Social management is the same shape of work across two to four surfaces, with original creative and short-form video on top — so it can’t price below that, and the entry tier has to clear Care Plus ($150) or the ladder stops making sense.

That puts the entry at $250/mo. It’s the first Veer product above the “small monthly” band, and deliberately so: social is the first service line where the labour is genuinely recurring rather than mostly automated upkeep.

02 One-time

Social Rescue — $300

Priced identically to Google Profile Rescue, and for the same reason: a week of fixing everything an audit found, at a fixed price, before any retainer starts. Matching the two keeps the bundle maths legible and means a client who bought one already knows what the other is. Sold on its own — they can take the Rescue and run the account themselves.

Found and fixed

  • Profile audit scored out of 100 — same rubric shape as the GBP audit, so before/after is a number, not an opinion
  • Handles, names, bios rewritten — one voice, keyword-aware, full character count used
  • Profile and cover art sized per platform, plus Instagram highlight covers
  • Grid cleanup plan — what to archive, pin, keep
  • 30-day re-score so they watch the number move

Built to hand over

  • One link page on their own domain — the links.html pattern, not a service they rent
  • Six post templates in their colours and type, locked as a Canva brand template
  • Five content pillars — written down, so no month starts from nothing
  • A 30-day calendar ready to publish at the tier’s cadence
  • Twelve hashtag sets researched for their market and city, grouped by pillar

04 Margin

Every tier is built to $50/hr

The constraint the whole line rests on

That budget only holds because of batching. A month of captions written in one sitting is about 90 minutes; the same sixteen written one at a time across four weeks is closer to five hours. If the monthly cycle stops being batched, every tier loses money.

05 Production

Nothing new to build — a client to add to a system already running four brands

Veer Internal/Social Engine/ already drives Veer, MDRN, Camden Shapeworks and Common Press. build-desk.py regenerates Social Desk from one <brand>-posts.json per brand, resolves platform routing per post, and flags any media reference missing from disk. Statuses run pending → approved → scheduled → posted, and the app keeps its own browser state so a rebuild re-seeds the archive without wiping what’s already marked posted.

Onboarding a client, in full

Add <client>-posts.json, register it in the EXTRA map in build-desk.py, point mediaRoot at Clients/<Name>/, re-run the build. That’s it — the same shape MDRN and Camden already use.

The monthly cycle

The client-facing promise is the one the website service already makes: you do almost nothing.

  1. D1–2

    Intake

    One 15-minute voice note or call, plus a photo dump into a shared Drive folder. Capped at 30 minutes of the client’s time. Any format, any quality — the cropping and cleanup is Veer’s problem.

  2. D2–3

    Batch write — the whole month in one pass

    Claude drafts every caption straight into <client>-posts.json from the five pillars, the client’s voice sample and whatever they sent — caption, hashtag set, platform routing and media refs all in the same pass. This is the biggest lever in the service and the reason the margin works.

  3. D3–4

    Media

    Graphics from the locked Canva brand template. Short-form video and motion cuts through Higgsfield, which also handles background removal and upscaling on rough client photos. Logo and vector work through the Vectorizer in this repo. Client photos cropped per platform.

  4. D4

    Route and review — one link

    build-desk.py regenerates the Desk with per-post routing, then an approval page deploys under the existing veer-proposals Vercel project. The client gets one link and taps approve or comments per post. No email threads, no PDF markup, no app to install — and nothing new to maintain.

  5. D5

    Schedule

    Approved posts queued for the month, statuses tracked in the Desk.

  6. →

    Engagement, ongoing

    Comments, DMs and reviews triaged at the tier’s SLA. Anything needing the owner’s own answer gets forwarded, not guessed at.

  7. WK4

    Report

    What posted, what performed, follower and engagement delta, next month’s angle — plus the re-scored profile number. Same “watch it climb” framing as Google Care, because it’s the same promise: measurable before and after.

Why the approval link earns its keep

It’s the difference between a service the client trusts and one they micromanage — one tap per post, on their phone, between jobs. It’s also a work sample: a client looking at a clean approval page is looking at evidence of why they hired a design studio instead of a cousin with Canva.

06 Fine print

The boundaries that make this deliverable

Guaranteed, in writing

  • The cadence — posts land on schedule
  • The reply times — per the tier’s SLA
  • The creative quality — on-brand, from locked templates
  • A measurable profile score before and after
  • The client owns everything — accounts, assets, captions; all handed back on exit
  • 30 days’ notice either way, and the final approved queue is delivered scheduled and ready regardless of who ended it

Never promised, never included

  • Follower counts, reach, or a post going viral. Nobody can promise those — they depend on the algorithm and on competitors. Same rule as the published GBP promise: the fixable things get fixed, and the score is visible before and after
  • Purchased followers or engagement of any kind
  • Crisis and PR response
  • Paid spend on Veer’s card
  • Anything needing the owner’s own voice on a legal or personnel matter
Operational terms

Delegated access only — Meta Business Suite partner access or equivalent. Never a shared password, never posting from a personal login.

Approval deadline: 3 business days. Past that the queue publishes as drafted — otherwise one unresponsive week kills a month’s cadence. Unused posts don’t roll over: the cadence is the product, a backlog isn’t.

07 Open

Four calls that are yours, not mine

  1. Is $250 the right entry, or does a $150 “posts only” tier belong below it to convert $500-website clients who balk? Risk: $150 for 8 posts is $30/hr once graphics are in, and it cannibalises Care Plus.
  2. Local SMB only, or does Social Studio get pitched to e-comm? Camden and Common Press prove the pipeline handles product catalogues, and an e-comm account carries $1,500+/mo — but that’s a different sales conversation than a restaurant.
  3. Content capture day at $250 — is two hours on-site the right shape, or a half-day at $400?
  4. Should Social Rescue bundle into Digital Presence Rescue ($750 website + GBP) as a three-way at, say, $950?
Once those are settled

Tier table into pricing.html; service names and rates into the veer-proposal skill’s known-services list (so no proposal ever invents a social price the way Google Profile Setup once got invented); and a customer-facing social.html built on the care.html pattern.
