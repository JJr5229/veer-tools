Social Care

A build-your-quote matrix for managing a client's social accounts — account setup, organic content, and paid ads priced on separate axes, because they scale separately. Every rate is derived from the Veer rate card already published, so the two pages never contradict each other. Send a prospect the questionnaire and it fills the calculator for you. Working notes in SPEC.md.

01 Where it fits

The rung that was advertised and never priced

The published rate card already ends on this promise — step 3 of the “What’s next” ladder reads “AI working behind the scenes — chases invoices, replies to reviews, and drafts your posts.” Social isn’t a new direction. It’s the rung clients are already being shown.

The number that sets the floor

Google Care is $85/mo for 8 posts to a single surface, with a 48-hour reply SLA and a monthly re-score. Social management is the same shape of work across two to four surfaces, with original creative and video on top — so it can’t price below that, and the organic entry has to clear Google Care Plus ($150) or the ladder stops making sense. That puts the entry at $250/mo.

02 The matrix

Three axes, because one tier number can’t carry the quote

A quote has to answer three questions that scale independently. Fold them into one tier price and a client with one live Instagram pays the same as a client with four accounts that don’t exist yet — wrong in both directions.

Axis A

Accounts

Does it exist, or am I building it? Scales with how many accounts there are and what state each one is in.

One-time
Axis B

Organic

How much content, pushed to how many surfaces? Volume sets the tier; platform count is priced on top of it.

Monthly
Axis C

Paid ads

How many ad platforms, at what spend on each? Entirely separate from organic — its own setup, fee and risk.

One-time setup + monthly
The decomposition that makes a matrix possible

Client-level strategy work happens once, no matter how many accounts there are. Pillars, templates, calendar, hashtag research and the voice guide are a fixed cost. Account work multiplies. Charging one number for both is what made a flat “$300 Social Rescue” unable to answer what if they have four accounts?

So the Rescue splits: Foundation (once) + per-account work (multiplies).

03 Axis A

Accounts — one-time

Social Foundation — $300, charged once

Required on every engagement. Holds everything that isn’t per-account: five content pillars, a voice guide taken off one 15-minute call, six post templates locked as a Canva brand template, a 30-day calendar, twelve hashtag sets researched for their market, a link page on their own domain — plus the first account brought to standard.

$300 matches Google Profile Rescue exactly, and the parallel is precise: GBP Rescue is scoped to one location, extra locations +$150 each. Foundation is scoped to one account.

Each additional account

State of the accountPriceWhat it takes

Both numbers already exist on the rate card

$150 is the published extra-location rate. $75 is the published review-backlog rate. Nothing was invented to make this table work.

Why building costs more than fixing. A neglected account is already populated — the work is corrective and desk-bound. A new one needs handle research (the good ones are taken), business verification that runs on Meta’s clock and needs documents from the client, asset linkage, and a starter grid, because an account with zero posts converts worse than no account at all. The starter grid alone is most of the delta.

Native vs mirrored — the distinction that makes platform count priceable

Mirrored platform — the same content, reformatted: aspect ratio, caption length, hashtag conventions. Facebook mirroring Instagram. Cheap because the thinking is already done.+$50/mo
Native platform — its own content, adapted to that platform's culture. TikTok is not LinkedIn; a post that works on one reads as a tourist on the other.+$125/mo
Extra location — matches the published GBP add-on+$150/mo
Extra reel or short beyond the tier+$60 ea
This replaces the flat “+$75 per platform”

A client adding Facebook alongside Instagram is buying something genuinely cheap. A client adding TikTok is not. One flat rate lied about both.

05 Axis C

Paid ads — setup and monthly

Separated from organic entirely. Meta (Facebook + Instagram together) counts as one ad platform, because it is one ads account. Google, TikTok, LinkedIn and Pinterest each count separately.

Why flat at the bottom, and why it drops at the top

15% of $600 is $90 — less than the cost of opening the account and looking at it properly. A percentage-only model quietly loses money on every small client, and small local businesses are the whole book. The $200 floor is what the work costs. At the other end, above $10k spend grows faster than the work does, so 12% keeps a scaling client from being punished for scaling.

The two hard rules on ads

1. The client pays spend on their own card. Veer never fronts ad spend and never holds client billing on a Veer payment method. Fronting spend turns a $550/mo retainer into an unsecured loan.

2. No performance promise. Ever. No ROAS, no cost-per-lead, no cost-per-acquisition figure in any proposal or conversation. What’s guaranteed is that campaigns are built correctly, watched, and reported honestly. The numbers the auction returns are not Veer’s to promise — and a quoted CPL that misses is the fastest way to lose a client in a city this size.

06 Intake

The questionnaire that fills this in

intake.html is the client-facing questionnaire — light Veer brand, about five minutes, almost entirely tapping rather than typing. It saves to the phone as they go, only shows the ads block if they say they want ads, and ends with a copy button and a mailto. It needs no backend to work; set the ENDPOINT constant at the top of its script and it POSTs JSON instead. intake-questions.md is the same thing as a call script.

Six questions carry the entire price. Everything else shapes the work, not the number.

How many platforms aren’t marked “not for us” Axis B. Anything past the tier’s included slots is an extra platform

Two things the form deliberately won’t decide

Native vs mirrored. Every overflow platform defaults to mirrored (+$50), the cheaper call. Whether a client genuinely needs native content on TikTok (+$125) comes out of the conversation, not a checkbox.

The tier. Cadence gives a starting point. If someone picks “2 a week” but wants online sales and video, Care can’t deliver it — the form’s job is to surface that mismatch, not resolve it.

      The constraint the whole line rests on

      The organic budget only holds because of batching. A month of captions written in one sitting is about 90 minutes; the same sixteen written one at a time across four weeks is closer to five hours. If the monthly cycle stops being batched, every tier loses money.

      10 Production

      Nothing new to build — a client to add to a system already running four brands

      Veer Internal/Social Engine/ already drives Veer, MDRN, Camden Shapeworks and Common Press. build-desk.py regenerates Social Desk from one <brand>-posts.json per brand, resolves platform routing per post, and flags any media reference missing from disk. Statuses run pending → approved → scheduled → posted, and the app keeps its own browser state so a rebuild re-seeds the archive without wiping what’s already marked posted.

      Onboarding a client, in full

      Add <client>-posts.json, register it in the EXTRA map in build-desk.py, point mediaRoot at Clients/<Name>/, re-run the build. The same shape MDRN and Camden already use.

      The platforms array on each post is what makes axis B auditable — mirrored platforms are extra entries on an existing post, native ones are their own posts. The month’s invoice is derivable from the file.

      The monthly cycle

      The client-facing promise is the one the website service already makes: you do almost nothing.

      1. D1–2

        Intake

        One 15-minute voice note or call, plus a photo dump into a shared Drive folder. Capped at 30 minutes of the client’s time. Any format, any quality — the cropping and cleanup is Veer’s problem.

      2. D2–3

        Batch write — the whole month in one pass

        Claude drafts every caption straight into <client>-posts.json from the five pillars, the voice guide and whatever they sent — caption, hashtag set, platform routing and media refs in the same pass. The biggest lever in the service and the reason the margin works.

      3. D3–4

        Media

        Graphics from the locked Canva brand template. Short-form video and motion cuts through Higgsfield, which also handles background removal and upscaling on rough client photos. Logo and vector work through the Vectorizer in this repo.

      4. D4

        Route and review — one link

        build-desk.py regenerates the Desk with per-post routing, then an approval page deploys under the existing veer-proposals Vercel project. The client gets one link and taps approve or comments per post. No email threads, no PDF markup, no app to install — and nothing new to maintain.

      5. D5

        Schedule

        Approved posts queued for the month, statuses tracked in the Desk.

      6. WKLY

        Ads, if running

        Budget and bid checks, kill the losers, scale the winners, creative refresh when frequency climbs. Logged so the monthly report can show what changed and why.

      7. WK4

        Report

        What posted, what performed, follower and engagement delta, the re-scored profile number, and — if ads ran — spend, results and what changed. Same “watch it climb” framing as Google Care: measurable before and after.

      Why the approval link earns its keep

      It’s the difference between a service the client trusts and one they micromanage — one tap per post, on their phone, between jobs. It’s also a work sample: a client looking at a clean approval page is looking at evidence of why they hired a design studio instead of a cousin with Canva.

      11 Fine print

      The boundaries that make this deliverable

      Guaranteed, in writing

      • The cadence — posts land on schedule
      • The reply times — per the tier’s SLA
      • The creative quality — on-brand, from locked templates
      • Campaigns built correctly and actively watched
      • A measurable profile score before and after
      • The client owns everything — accounts, assets, captions, ad accounts. An account Veer created is theirs from day one, stated explicitly, because that is exactly where an ownership fight would start
      • 30 days’ notice either way, and the final approved queue delivered scheduled and ready regardless of who ended it

      Never promised, never included

      • Follower counts, reach, or a post going viral
      • Any advertising performance figure — no ROAS, CPL or CPA, ever. They depend on the auction and on competitors
      • Purchased followers or engagement of any kind
      • Ad spend fronted by Veer. If the client’s card fails, campaigns pause; Veer does not cover the gap
      • Crisis and PR response
      • Anything needing the owner’s own voice on a legal or personnel matter
      Operational terms

      Delegated access only — Meta Business Suite partner access or equivalent. Never a shared password, never posting from a personal login.

      Approval deadline: 3 business days. Past that the queue publishes as drafted — otherwise one unresponsive week kills a month’s cadence. Unused posts don’t roll over: the cadence is the product, a backlog isn’t.

      12 Open

      Five calls that are yours, not mine

      1. Do you want to own paid ads at all? The big one, and strategic rather than pricing. Ads carries a different liability profile — the client spends their own money on your recommendation, and a bad month is felt immediately in a way a mediocre post never is. Three honest options: build it in as specced; refer it out at a 15% referral with none of the exposure; or sell ad creative only at $60 a cut and let someone else run the account. The matrix supports all three — axis C just switches off.
      2. Is $250 the right organic entry, or does a $150 “posts only” tier belong below it to convert $500-website clients who balk? Risk: $150 for 8 posts is $30/hr once graphics are in, and it cannibalises Care Plus.
      3. Does the $0 row on axis A actually happen? A client whose accounts are genuinely healthy is rare, and advertising “free” invites them to argue every account is fine. It may be better to price every account at $75 minimum and let you waive it as a goodwill move.
      4. Local SMB only, or does Social Studio get pitched to e-comm? Camden and Common Press prove the pipeline handles product catalogues, and an e-comm account carries $1,500+/mo — a different sales conversation than a restaurant.
      5. Content capture day at $250 — two hours on-site, or a half-day at $400?
      Once those are settled

      Matrix into pricing.html; service names and rates into the veer-proposal skill’s known-services list (so no proposal ever invents a social price the way Google Profile Setup once got invented); and a customer-facing social.html built on the care.html pattern.
