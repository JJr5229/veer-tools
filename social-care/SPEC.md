# Veer Social Care — pricing, deliverables, production

Internal source of truth for the social media management service line. Written to sit
beside the existing Veer rate card, not on top of it — every number here is derived from
prices already published at `veersolutions.com/pricing`, so a client comparing the two
pages never finds a contradiction.

Status: **proposed, not yet published.** Once Jessie signs off, the matrix and the fine
print move into `Veer/Website/pricing.html` and the `veer-proposal` skill's
known-services list.

## 1. Where this fits in the existing ladder

The published rate card already ends on this promise. The "What's next" ladder on
`pricing.html` reads:

> **3. AI working behind the scenes** — Chases invoices, replies to reviews, and *drafts
> your posts* — added as you're ready.

So social is step 3 of a ladder clients are already being shown. It is not a new
direction; it's the rung that was advertised and never priced.

Existing monthly products, for reference:

| Product | Price | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Website Care | $25/mo | Hosting, SSL, backups, 30 min edits |
| Website Care Plus | $45/mo | + AI chat assistant, traffic insights, 60 min edits |
| Google Care | $85/mo | 8 posts/mo to GBP, review replies in 48h, monthly re-score |
| Google Care Plus | $150/mo | + review campaign, Tap-to-Review, 3 competitors, quarterly report |

**Google Care is the anchor that sets the floor.** $85/mo for 8 posts to a single surface,
with a 48-hour reply SLA and a monthly re-score. Social management is the same shape of
work across two to four surfaces, with original creative and short-form video on top. It
cannot price below that, and the entry tier has to clear Google Care Plus ($150) or the
ladder stops making sense. That puts the organic entry at **$250/mo**.

## 2. The three axes — why one tier number can't carry the quote

A quote has to answer three questions that scale independently. The first draft of this
spec folded all three into the tier price, which meant a client with one live Instagram
paid the same as a client with four accounts that don't exist yet. That's wrong in both
directions.

| Axis | Question | Scales with | Billing shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Accounts** | Does it exist, or am I building it? | Number of accounts, and their state | One-time |
| **B — Organic** | How much content, to how many surfaces? | Posts + video volume, then platform count | Monthly |
| **C — Ads** | How many ad platforms, at what spend? | Ad platforms, then spend per platform | One-time setup + monthly |

The critical decomposition: **client-level strategy work happens once, no matter how many
accounts there are.** Pillars, templates, calendar, hashtag research and the voice guide
are a fixed cost. Account work multiplies. Charging one number for both is what made the
old $300 "Social Rescue" unable to answer "what if they have four accounts?"

So the Rescue splits into **Foundation** (once) **+ per-account work** (multiplies).

## 3. Axis A — Accounts (one-time)

### Social Foundation — $300

Required on every engagement, charged once. Holds everything that isn't per-account:

- **Five content pillars** — the recurring subjects the account actually posts about,
  written down so no month starts from nothing.
- **Voice guide** — how this business sounds, captured from a 15-minute call, so batched
  captions come out sounding like them and not like a template.
- **Six post templates** in their colours and type, locked as a Canva brand template.
- **A 30-day calendar** ready to publish at the chosen tier's cadence.
- **Twelve hashtag sets** researched for their market and city, grouped by pillar.
- **One link page on their own domain** — the `links.html` pattern from the Trio
  Plant-Based build, not a service they rent.
- **The first account brought to standard** — whichever axis-A row below applies to it,
  included.

$300 matches **Google Profile Rescue** exactly, and the parallel is precise: GBP Rescue is
scoped to *one location*, with extra locations at +$150 each. Foundation is scoped to one
account, with extra accounts priced below.

### Per additional account

| State of the account | Price | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Already live, healthy** | **$0** | Verify access and routing. Nothing to fix. |
| **Already live, needs work** | **+$75** | Audit + score out of 100, bio and handle rewritten, links wired, covers, grid cleanup plan, 30-day re-score. |
| **Doesn't exist yet** | **+$150** | Handle availability research and claim, business verification, Meta Business Suite / asset linkage, 2FA, category and attributes, profile and cover art, **plus a 6-post starter grid** so it doesn't look abandoned on day one. |

Both numbers already exist on the published rate card — **$150** is the extra-location
rate, **$75** is the review-backlog rate. Nothing new was invented.

**Why new costs more than fixing.** A neglected account is already populated; the work is
corrective and mostly desk-bound. A new account needs handle research (the good ones are
taken), business verification that runs on Meta's clock and needs documents from the
client, asset linkage, and a starter grid — because an account with zero posts converts
worse than no account at all. The starter grid alone is most of the delta.

## 4. Axis B — Organic content (monthly)

The tier sets **volume**. Platform reach is priced separately, because posting the same
sixteen posts to two platforms and to five platforms is not the same job.

| | **Social Care** | **Social Care Plus** *(rec.)* | **Social Studio** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Price** | **$250/mo** | **$550/mo** | **$950/mo** |
| Posts per month | 8 (2/wk) | 16 (4/wk) | 24 (6/wk) |
| Short-form video | — | 4 reels + 8 stories | 8 reels + 2 ad cuts + 16 stories |
| Platforms included | 1 native + 1 mirrored | 2 native + 1 mirrored | 3 native + 1 mirrored |
| Comment + DM replies | 48h, weekdays | 24h, weekdays | 24h, incl. weekends |
| Review replies | — | Included | Included |
| Content capture day | — | 1 per quarter | 1 per month |
| Monthly report | Yes | + 3 competitors tracked | + quarterly strategy call |
| Revisions | 1 round | 1 round | Unlimited in-cycle |

### Native vs mirrored — the distinction that makes platform count priceable

| | Price | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Mirrored platform** | **+$50/mo** | The same content, reformatted — aspect ratio, caption length, hashtag conventions. Facebook mirroring Instagram. Cheap because the thinking is already done. |
| **Native platform** | **+$125/mo** | Its own content, adapted to that platform's culture. TikTok is not LinkedIn; a post that works on one reads as a tourist on the other. This is real additional creative work. |

Replacing the old flat "+$75/platform" with this split is the single most useful pricing
change in this revision. A client adding Facebook alongside Instagram is buying something
genuinely cheap; a client adding TikTok is not, and a flat rate lied about both.

Extra location: **+$150/mo** (matches the published GBP add-on).
Extra reel or short beyond the tier: **+$60 each**.

## 5. Axis C — Paid ads

Ads is separated from organic entirely. It has its own setup, its own monthly fee, its own
risk profile, and — importantly — its own honesty problem, since the client is spending
their own money on Veer's recommendation.

### Ads setup (one-time, per ad platform)

| | Price | What it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **First ad platform** | **$250** | Business Manager / ads account, pixel or CAPI installed and firing, domain verification, conversion events defined, three starter audiences built, payment and billing thresholds set. |
| **Each additional ad platform** | **+$150** | Same shape, less discovery — the audience definitions and creative already exist. |

Meta (Facebook + Instagram together) counts as **one** ad platform, because it is one ads
account. Google, TikTok, LinkedIn and Pinterest each count separately.

### Ads management (monthly, per ad platform)

Priced on that platform's own monthly spend:

| Monthly spend on that platform | Management fee |
|---|---|
| Under $1,000 | **$200/mo flat** |
| $1,000 – $2,499 | **$300/mo flat** |
| $2,500 – $9,999 | **15% of spend** |
| $10,000 and up | **12% of spend** |

- **Each additional ad platform: −25%** off its own calculated fee. Audiences, creative
  and reporting are shared across platforms, so the second platform genuinely costs less
  than the first. The discount applies to the cheaper platforms, so the client's biggest
  channel is never the discounted one.
- **Two ad creatives per platform per month included.** Extras at $60 each — same rate as
  an extra reel, because it's the same work.
- **Why flat at the bottom.** 15% of $600 is $90, which is less than the cost of opening
  the account and looking at it properly. A percentage-only model quietly loses money on
  every small client, and small local businesses are the whole book. The $200 floor is
  what the work actually costs.
- **Why the rate drops at $10k.** Above that, spend grows faster than the work does. A
  client scaling up shouldn't be punished for it, and 12% is still well inside market.

### The two hard rules on ads

1. **The client pays spend on their own card.** Veer never fronts ad spend, and never
   holds a client's billing on a Veer payment method. This is not negotiable — fronting
   spend turns a $550/mo retainer into an unsecured loan.
2. **No performance promise. Ever.** No ROAS, no cost-per-lead, no cost-per-acquisition
   figure in any proposal or conversation. Same rule and same wording style as the
   published GBP promise: what's guaranteed is that the campaigns are built correctly,
   watched, and reported honestly — the numbers the auction returns are not Veer's to
   promise. A quoted CPL that misses is the fastest way to lose a client and a reputation
   in a city this size.

## 8. Internal margin check

Organic targets **$50/hr** blended:

| Tier | Price | Budgeted hrs/mo | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social Care | $250 | ~5 | $50/hr |
| Social Care Plus | $550 | ~11 | $50/hr |
| Social Studio | $950 | ~19 | $50/hr |

Ads targets **$60–75/hr**, deliberately higher — it carries more skill, more client
anxiety, and more downside if it goes wrong:

| Scenario | Fee | Est. hrs/mo | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta, $800 spend | $200 | ~3.5 | ~$57/hr |
| Meta, $1,500 spend | $300 | ~4.5 | ~$67/hr |
| Meta, $4,000 spend | $600 | ~8 | ~$75/hr |

One-time work at $50/hr: Foundation ~6 hrs, new account ~3 hrs, existing tune-up ~1.5 hrs,
Meta ads setup ~5 hrs.

**The constraint the whole line rests on:** the organic budget only holds because of
batching. A month of captions written in one sitting is about 90 minutes; the same sixteen
written one at a time across four weeks is closer to five hours. If the monthly cycle
stops being batched, every tier loses money.

Breakdown for Social Care Plus (11 hrs):

| Step | Hours |
|---|---|
| Intake + reading the photo dump | 0.5 |
| Batch caption writing (all 16, one pass) | 1.5 |
| Graphics from locked templates (16) | 2.5 |
| Short-form video (4 cuts) | 2.5 |
| Route, build approval page, handle revisions | 1.5 |
| Scheduling | 0.5 |
| Engagement — comments, DMs, reviews | 1.5 |
| Monthly report | 0.5 |

## 9. Production — how this actually gets made

Everything below runs on tooling that already exists. There is no new system to build
before the first client; there is a client to add to a system already running four brands
(Veer, MDRN, Camden Shapeworks, Common Press).

### The engine: Social Desk

`Veer Internal/Social Engine/` holds the pipeline:

- `posts.json` and one `<brand>-posts.json` per brand — the content archive. Each post is
  `{ seq, brand, status, product, caption, hashtags, media, mediaRoot, platforms, type }`.
- `desk.template.html` — the app shell.
- `build-desk.py` — regenerates `Social Desk.html` from the seed data, resolves platform
  routing, and reports any media reference that doesn't exist on disk.

The Desk keeps its own state in the browser, so re-running the build re-seeds the archive
without wiping anything already marked posted. Statuses move
`pending → approved → scheduled → posted`.

**A new client is a new brand in the Desk.** Add `<client>-posts.json`, register it in the
`EXTRA` map in `build-desk.py`, point `mediaRoot` at `Clients/<Name>/`, re-run the build.
That's the whole onboarding — the same shape MDRN and Camden already use.

The `platforms` array on each post is what makes axis B billable and auditable: mirrored
platforms are extra entries on an existing post, native ones are their own posts. The
month's invoice is derivable from the file.

### Setup week

1. Add the client as a brand in Social Desk; create `Clients/<Name>/` with the
   `assets/real/` and `_deliverables/` layout already used for Trio Plant-Based.
2. Axis A work per account — audit and score the live ones, build the new ones.
3. Build the six post templates as a Canva brand template (via the Canva connector, so
   every later graphic is `create-design-from-brand-template` rather than a fresh design).
4. Write the five pillars, the voice guide and the 30-day calendar into
   `<client>-posts.json`.
5. Request delegated access — Meta Business Suite partner access, never a shared password.
6. If ads: Business Manager, pixel/CAPI, domain verification, conversion events, three
   starter audiences. Confirm the client's own card is on the ad account before anything
   goes live.

### The monthly cycle

The client-facing promise is the same one the website service makes: *you do almost
nothing.*

**Day 1–2 — Intake.** One 15-minute voice note or call, plus a photo dump into a shared
Drive folder. Capped at 30 minutes of the client's time. Any format, any quality — the
cropping and cleanup is Veer's problem.

**Day 2–3 — Batch write.** Claude drafts the entire month's captions in one pass, straight
into `<client>-posts.json`, working from the five pillars, the voice guide, and whatever
they sent. Every post gets its caption, hashtag set, platform routing and media refs in
the same pass. *This is the single biggest lever in the service and the reason the margin
works.*

**Day 3–4 — Media.** Graphics from the locked Canva templates. Short-form video and motion
cuts through Higgsfield, which also handles background removal and upscaling on rough
client photos. Logo and vector work through the Vectorizer in this repo. Client photos
cropped per platform.

**Day 4 — Route and review.** `build-desk.py` regenerates the Desk with per-post platform
routing. Export an approval page and deploy it under the existing `veer-proposals` Vercel
project — the client gets **one link**, and taps approve or leaves a comment per post. No
email threads, no PDF markup, no app to install. Same infrastructure as the proposals, so
there is nothing new to maintain.

**Day 5 — Schedule.** Approved posts get queued for the month. Statuses tracked in the
Desk.

**Ongoing — Engagement.** Comments, DMs and reviews triaged at the tier's SLA. Anything
that needs the owner's own answer gets forwarded, not guessed at.

**Ads, weekly if running.** Budget and bid checks, kill the losers, scale the winners,
creative refresh when frequency climbs. Logged so the monthly report can show what changed
and why.

**Last week — Report.** What posted, what performed, follower and engagement delta, the
re-scored profile number, and — if ads ran — spend, results and what changed. Same "watch
it climb" framing as Google Care, because it's the same promise: measurable before and
after.

### Why the approval link matters commercially

It is the difference between a service the client trusts and a service they micromanage.
One tap per post, on their phone, between jobs. It is also a work sample — a client
looking at a clean approval page is looking at evidence of why they hired a design studio
instead of a cousin with Canva.

## 10. Fine print — the boundaries that make this deliverable

- **The client owns everything.** Every account, every asset, every caption, every ad
  account. Veer works from delegated access and hands it all back on exit. An account
  Veer *created* is still the client's from day one — this must be explicit in writing,
  because a new account built by Veer is exactly where an ownership fight would start.
- **Delegated access only.** Meta Business Suite partner access or equivalent. Veer never
  takes a shared password, and never posts from a personal login.
- **Approval deadline: 3 business days.** Past that, the queue publishes as drafted —
  otherwise an unresponsive week kills a month's cadence.
- **Unused posts don't roll over.** The cadence is the product; a backlog isn't.
- **30 days' notice either way.** The final month's approved queue is delivered on exit,
  scheduled and ready, regardless of who ended it.
- **Ad spend is the client's, on the client's card.** Veer never fronts it. If the card
  fails, campaigns pause; Veer does not cover the gap.
- **Not included:** crisis and PR response, purchased followers or engagement of any kind,
  and anything requiring the owner's own voice on a legal or personnel matter.
- **What is guaranteed:** the cadence, the reply times, the creative quality, correctly
  built and actively watched campaigns, and a measurable profile score before and after.
- **What is not guaranteed — in writing, every time:** follower counts, reach, a post
  going viral, or any advertising performance figure (ROAS, CPL, CPA). Nobody can promise
  those; they depend on the platform's auction and on competitors. Same rule, same wording
  style, as the published GBP promise: *what we promise is that the fixable things get
  fixed, and that you can see your score before and after.*

## 11. Open questions for Jessie

1. **Do you want to own paid ads at all?** This is the big one, and it's a strategic
   question rather than a pricing one. Ads is a different service with a different
   liability profile — the client is spending their own money on your recommendation, and
   a bad month is felt immediately in a way a mediocre post never is. Three honest
   options: build it in as specced; **refer it out and take a 15% referral** with none of
   the exposure; or **offer ad creative only** (you cut the assets at $60 each, someone
   else runs the account). The matrix supports all three — the ads axis just switches off.
2. **Is $250 the right organic entry, or does a $150 "posts only" tier belong below it**
   to convert $500-website clients who balk? Risk: $150 for 8 posts is $30/hr once
   graphics are in, and it cannibalises Care Plus.
3. **Does the $0 row on axis A actually happen?** A client whose accounts are genuinely
   healthy is rare, and "free" invites them to argue every account is fine. It may be
   better to price every account at $75 minimum and let you waive it as a goodwill move
   rather than advertise a zero.
4. **Local SMB only, or does Social Studio get pitched to e-comm?** Camden and Common
   Press prove the pipeline handles product catalogues, and an e-comm account carries
   $1,500+/mo — but that's a different sales conversation than a restaurant.
5. **Content capture day at $250** — two hours on-site, or a half-day at $400?

Once these are settled: matrix into `pricing.html`, service names and rates into the
`veer-proposal` skill's known-services list, and a customer-facing `social.html` built on
the `care.html` pattern.
