# Veer Social Care — pricing, deliverables, production

Internal source of truth for the social media management service line. Written to sit
beside the existing Veer rate card, not on top of it — every number here is derived from
prices already published at `veersolutions.com/pricing`, so a client comparing the two
pages never finds a contradiction.

Status: **proposed, not yet published.** Nothing here is on the customer-facing pricing
page yet. Once Jessie signs off, the tier table and the fine print move into
`Veer/Website/pricing.html` and the `veer-proposal` skill's known-services list.

## 1. Where this fits in the existing ladder

The published rate card already ends with a promise that this service fulfils. The
"What's next" ladder on `pricing.html` reads:

> **3. AI working behind the scenes** — Chases invoices, replies to reviews, and *drafts
> your posts* — added as you're ready.

So social is step 3 of a ladder clients are already being shown. It is not a new
direction; it's the rung that was advertised and never priced.

Existing monthly products, for reference:

| Product | Price | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Website Care | $25/mo | Hosting, SSL, backups, 30 min edits |
| Website Care Plus | $45/mo | + AI chat assistant, traffic insights, 60 min edits |
| Google Care | $85/mo | 8 posts/mo to GBP, review replies in 48h, photos, monthly re-score |
| Google Care Plus | $150/mo | + review campaign, Tap-to-Review, 3 competitors, quarterly report |

**Google Care is the anchor that sets the floor.** It is $85/mo for 8 posts a month to a
single surface, with a 48-hour reply SLA and a monthly re-score. Social media management
is the same shape of work across two to four surfaces, with original creative and
short-form video on top. It cannot be priced below that, and the entry tier has to clear
Google Care Plus ($150) or the ladder stops making sense.

**Consequence: the social entry tier starts at $250/mo.** That is a real step up from
$150, and it is the first Veer product priced above the "small monthly" psychological
band. It is deliberate — social is the first service line where the labour is genuinely
recurring rather than mostly automated upkeep.

## 2. One-time: Social Rescue — $300

Priced identically to **Google Profile Rescue ($300)**, and for the same reason: it is a
week of fixing everything an audit found, on a fixed price, before any retainer starts.
Keeping the two Rescues at the same number means the bundle maths stay legible and a
client who bought one already understands what the other is.

Deliverables:

- **Profile audit, scored out of 100** — each platform scored on the same rubric the
  GBP audit uses, so the before/after is a number and not an opinion.
- **Handles, names and bios rewritten** — one voice across every platform, keyword-aware,
  full character count used.
- **Every link wired to one link page** — built on the client's own domain (the
  `links.html` pattern from the Trio Plant-Based build), never a third-party service
  they rent.
- **Profile and cover art** — sized correctly per platform, plus highlight covers for
  Instagram.
- **Six post templates** in the client's colours and type — locked as a Canva brand
  template so every month's graphics are consistent without being redesigned.
- **Five content pillars** — the recurring subjects the account will actually post about,
  written down so the month's content is never invented from nothing.
- **A 30-day calendar**, ready to publish, at the cadence of whichever tier they pick.
- **Twelve hashtag sets** researched for their market and their city, grouped by pillar.
- **Grid cleanup plan** — what to archive, what to pin, what to keep.
- **30-day re-score** so the client sees the audit number move.

Sold on its own. A client can take the Rescue and run the account themselves.

## 5. Internal margin check

Target blended rate is **$50/hr**, which is what every tier is actually built to:

| Tier | Price | Budgeted hours/mo | Effective rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social Care | $250 | ~5 | $50/hr |
| Social Care Plus | $550 | ~11 | $50/hr |
| Social Studio | $950 | ~19 | $50/hr |

That budget only holds because of the batching described below. A month of captions
written in one sitting is roughly 90 minutes; the same sixteen captions written one at a
time across four weeks is closer to five hours. **If the monthly cycle stops being
batched, every tier loses money.** That is the operational constraint this whole service
line rests on.

Rough breakdown for Social Care Plus (11 hrs):

| Step | Hours |
|---|---|
| Intake + reading the photo dump | 0.5 |
| Batch caption writing (all 16, one pass) | 1.5 |
| Graphics from locked templates (16) | 2.5 |
| Short-form video (4 cuts) | 2.5 |
| Route, build approval page, handle revisions | 1.5 |
| Scheduling | 0.5 |
| Engagement — comments, DMs, reviews | 1.5 |
| Monthly report | 0.5 |

## 6. Production — how this actually gets made

Everything below runs on tooling that already exists. There is no new system to build
before the first client; there is a client to add to a system already running four
brands (Veer, MDRN, Camden Shapeworks, Common Press).

### The engine: Social Desk

`Veer Internal/Social Engine/` holds the pipeline:

- `posts.json` and one `<brand>-posts.json` per brand — the content archive. Each post is
  `{ seq, brand, status, product, caption, hashtags, media, mediaRoot, platforms, type }`.
- `desk.template.html` — the app shell.
- `build-desk.py` — regenerates `Social Desk.html` from the seed data, resolves platform
  routing, and reports any media reference that doesn't exist on disk.

The Desk keeps its own state in the browser, so re-running the build re-seeds the archive
without wiping anything already marked posted. Statuses move
`pending → approved → scheduled → posted`.

**A new client is a new brand in the Desk.** Add `<client>-posts.json`, register it in the
`EXTRA` map in `build-desk.py`, point `mediaRoot` at `Clients/<Name>/`, re-run the build.
That's the whole onboarding — the same shape MDRN and Camden already use.

### Setup week (the Rescue)

1. Add the client as a brand in Social Desk; create `Clients/<Name>/` with the
   `assets/real/` and `_deliverables/` layout already used for Trio Plant-Based.
2. Audit each profile against the rubric, score it, write the findings.
3. Build the six post templates as a Canva brand template (via the Canva connector, so
   every later graphic is `create-design-from-brand-template` rather than a fresh design).
4. Write the five pillars and the 30-day calendar into `<client>-posts.json`.
5. Request delegated access — Meta Business Suite partner access, never a shared
   password. (See boundaries below.)

### The monthly cycle

The client-facing promise is the same one the website service already makes: *you do
almost nothing.*

**Day 1–2 — Intake.** One 15-minute voice note or call, plus a photo dump into a shared
Drive folder. Capped at 30 minutes of the client's time. Any format, any quality — the
cropping and cleanup is Veer's problem.

**Day 2–3 — Batch write.** Claude drafts the entire month's captions in one pass,
straight into `<client>-posts.json`, working from the five pillars, the client's voice
sample, and whatever they sent. Every post gets its caption, hashtag set, platform
routing and media refs in the same pass. *This is the single biggest lever in the whole
service and the reason the margin works.*

**Day 3–4 — Media.** Graphics from the locked Canva templates. Short-form video and
motion cuts through Higgsfield, which also handles background removal and upscaling on
rough client photos. Logo and vector work through the Vectorizer in this repo. Client
photos cropped per platform.

**Day 4 — Route and review.** `build-desk.py` regenerates the Desk with per-post platform
routing. Export an approval page and deploy it under the existing
`veer-proposals` Vercel project — the client gets **one link**, and taps approve or leaves
a comment per post. No email threads, no PDF markup, no app to install. Same
infrastructure as the proposals, so there is nothing new to maintain.

**Day 5 — Schedule.** Approved posts get queued for the month. Statuses tracked in the
Desk.

**Ongoing — Engagement.** Comments, DMs and reviews triaged at the tier's SLA. Anything
that needs the owner's own answer gets forwarded, not guessed at.

**Last week — Report.** What posted, what performed, follower and engagement delta, and
next month's angle — plus the re-scored profile number. Same "watch it climb" framing as
Google Care, because it's the same promise: the score is measurable before and after.

### Why the approval link matters commercially

It is the difference between a service the client trusts and a service they micromanage.
One tap per post, on their phone, between jobs. It is also a work sample — a client
looking at a clean approval page is looking at evidence of why they hired a design studio
instead of a cousin with Canva.

## 7. Fine print — the boundaries that make this deliverable

- **The client owns everything.** Every account, every asset, every caption. Veer works
  from delegated access and hands it all back on exit.
- **Delegated access only.** Meta Business Suite partner access or equivalent. Veer never
  takes a shared password, and never posts from a personal login.
- **Approval deadline: 3 business days.** Past that, the queue publishes as drafted —
  otherwise an unresponsive week kills a month's cadence.
- **Unused posts don't roll over.** The cadence is the product; a backlog isn't.
- **30 days' notice either way.** The final month's approved queue is delivered on exit,
  scheduled and ready, regardless of who ended it.
- **Not included:** crisis and PR response, paid spend on Veer's card, purchased
  followers or engagement of any kind, and anything requiring the owner's own voice on a
  legal or personnel matter.
- **What is guaranteed:** the cadence, the reply times, the creative quality, and a
  measurable profile score before and after.
- **What is not guaranteed — and this goes in writing, every time:** follower counts,
  reach, or a post going viral. Nobody can promise those; they depend on the platform's
  algorithm and on competitors. Same rule, same wording style, as the published GBP
  promise: *what we promise is that the fixable things get fixed, and that you can see
  your score before and after.*
